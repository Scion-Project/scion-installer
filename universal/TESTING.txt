Scion (iOS 16.5 on Exynos 9810) - one installer for Galaxy S9, S9+ and Note9
===========================================================================
PRIVATE: this zip contains files built from an iOS IPSW. Do not repost or upload it anywhere.

Phones: Galaxy S9 SM-G960F, S9+ SM-G965F, Note9 SM-N960F (Exynos, F/FD models).
NOT Snapdragon, NOT the Korean N models: the installer refuses them.
Needs: unlocked bootloader + TWRP. Battery above 50%.

The installer works out which phone it is on, writes only SYSTEM and BOOT, verifies both, and refuses to touch
EFS, the modem or recovery. This zip does not write to USERDATA, so your Android data stays there.

What has been tested: this zip was installed for real on one Galaxy S9 (G960F), and booted, on the official TWRP.
Not tested: any S9+ or Note9 (nobody has one here), and the steps below that say so.

What you get depends on the phone:

GALAXY S9 - the full system, like the daily image. It runs until you end it.
  On:  all 8 cores, touch, keys, battery and charging, vibration, GPU, brightness slider, Wi-Fi (join from Settings),
       speakers, microphone, front camera in the Camera app, sensors (orientation, proximity), Bluetooth,
       Settings passcode, kernel log and console over USB.
  Not working: headphones, mobile network and calls, rear cameras.
  The hardware clock is not read in this build (it is left out to make room on the boot line), so the time may be
  wrong at first.

  /private/var on the S9, where iOS keeps your settings:
  - USERDATA holding Android's data, as on nearly every phone: /private/var lives in memory (about 1 GB) and is empty at
    every boot. Settings, Wi-Fi networks, the passcode and anything you save are gone after a restart. @VAR_MEMORY@
  - USERDATA already formatted HFS Plus, by the separate "format-userdata" installer (which erases Android's data; it is
    not this zip) or an earlier Scion: this zip uses that volume, and what is in it stays between restarts.

S9+ and NOTE9 - a minimal first run. Nobody has booted Scion on these phones yet.
  It starts on 2 cores with almost no drivers, mounts iOS from SYSTEM and, after 120 seconds, returns to TWRP by
  itself, keeping a log. The screen may stay black or frozen during the run: that is expected. Touch, keys, Wi-Fi and
  the rest are off. The log is what we need.

Steps
-----
1. In TWRP: Backup -> tick Boot, System and EFS -> swipe. Copy the TWRP folder to a computer.
   (EFS holds your IMEI - do not skip it.)
2. Put the zip where TWRP can read it, one of these. It is 2.6 GB. Do NOT use adb sideload: it cannot send a file over
   2 GB (the recovery gets a negative size, and nothing happens).
   a. A microSD card: copy the zip to it on a computer. In TWRP: Install -> Select Storage -> Micro SDCard. (@SD@)
   b. Internal storage, if TWRP shows it with 3 GB free: adb push scion-exynos9810-installer.zip /sdcard/ (@PUSH@)
   c. Neither (TWRP cannot read your data, or USERDATA is not Android's): into memory, which goes at the next restart.
      adb shell mount -o remount,size=3G /tmp
      adb push scion-exynos9810-installer.zip /tmp/
      adb shell twrp install /tmp/scion-exynos9810-installer.zip
      That is step 3 as well: watch for "Done" on the screen. This is the way that was tested.
   Optional: check it matches scion-exynos9810-installer.zip.sha256.
3. TWRP -> Install -> pick the zip -> swipe.
4. Reboot -> System.
   S9: wait for iOS. To leave: hold five fingers on the screen for 1.5 seconds (it restarts into TWRP and keeps the
       log), or force a restart with Vol Down + Power.
   S9+/Note9: wait about 3 minutes. It should come back to TWRP on its own.
   If it hangs (S9+/Note9: not back after 5 minutes): hold Vol Down + Power to force-restart, then immediately hold
   Vol Up + Bixby + Power to enter TWRP.
5. In TWRP (Advanced -> Terminal, or adb shell), save the logs. /sdcard may not be there (see step 2); then use your
   microSD card (/external_sd) or pull them over adb from /tmp.
     cp /sys/fs/pstore/* /sdcard/ 2>/dev/null; cat /proc/last_kmsg > /sdcard/last_kmsg.txt
   Send us everything that appears (pmsg-ramoops-0, console-ramoops*, last_kmsg.txt), plus a photo of the screen if
   anything showed during the run. Save the logs before doing anything else: they are gone after a full power-off.
6. To go back to Android: TWRP -> Restore -> your backup (Boot + System) -> swipe -> Reboot.

Passcode (S9)
-------------
You can set one in Settings, but it only gates the screen: nothing is encrypted with it.
Don't enable Erase Data under Passcode. This port does not support a wipe, and nobody has tested what it does here.
